Retail Store Analysis

&#x20;Business Analysis



If I look at this retail data from a practical business point of view, I would focus on a few key things:



1\. Stores needing more stock



First, I would compare total sales across all store locations.



Stores with higher sales are clearly getting more customers, so they should not run out of stock

Stores where products are selling fast should get extra inventory support



&#x20;This helps avoid lost sales due to stock shortage.



2\. Products that are not working



Next, I would check product performance.



Products with very low or almost no sales are not useful for the business

If they are not improving over time, they can be removed or replaced



This helps reduce waste and free up shelf space.



3\. Best time for promotions



Then I would look at sales trends over time.



Identify slow days, weeks, or months

These are the best times to run discounts or offers



&#x20;Promotions during slow periods can help increase customer traffic.



4\. Staff planning



Finally, I would compare store performance:



Busy stores need more staff to handle customers smoothly

Low-performing stores can manage with fewer staff or need training support



&#x20;This improves efficiency and customer experience.

Personal Finance Coaching — Coaching Algorithm

&#x20;Coaching Logic (Simple and Human-Friendly)



To build a personal finance advice feature, I would design the analysis like this:



1\. Identify high spending categories



First, I would calculate total spending by category and compare it with typical spending patterns.



If a category takes a large percentage of total spending, it is considered high

Example: If coffee or dining is too high compared to groceries or rent



&#x20;These categories need attention first



2\. Suggest savings opportunities



Then I would look for areas where money can be saved:



Frequent small expenses (coffee, snacks)

Non-essential spending (entertainment, dining out)

Subscriptions not used often



Suggest small changes like reducing frequency or setting limits



3\. Positive feedback for good habits



It is important to motivate users too:



If groceries or utilities are stable → appreciate that

If spending is controlled → highlight it as a good habit



This keeps users encouraged and engaged



4\. User-friendly advice format



Finally, I would present the output in a simple way:



Clear headings like “High Spending Areas”

Simple sentences (no technical terms)

Actionable advice like “Try reducing coffee spending by 20%”
